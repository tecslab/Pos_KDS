# 2. Business Context

## Restaurant Profile

Unlike premium restaurants that rely on large average ticket values, this restaurant competes by serving a high number of customers quickly.

Typical characteristics include:

* Average ticket: approximately USD 5
* Relatively small menu
* Continuous customer flow
* Short preparation times
* High staff coordination requirements

Operational efficiency directly impacts profitability.

Every minute saved during the order lifecycle increases the restaurant's capacity to serve additional customers.

---

## Restaurant Operation

Customers may either:

* Eat inside the restaurant.
* Purchase food to go.

The restaurant contains approximately:

* 20 dining tables.
* One dispatch window for takeout orders.

Waiters attend dining tables, while takeout customers are served directly through the dispatch window.

Kitchen personnel prepare food immediately after receiving orders.

Most ingredients are prepared before service begins.

Kitchen preparation primarily consists of:

* Heating proteins.
* Assembling products.
* Final presentation.

This allows the kitchen to produce food rapidly while handling a high number of simultaneous orders.

---

## Operational Challenges

The current operation faces several challenges common in high-volume restaurants.

### Communication delays

Kitchen staff and waiters require continuous communication regarding order readiness.

Without system support, prepared food may remain waiting before being delivered.

---

### Order visibility

Employees need immediate visibility into:

* Pending orders
* Orders under preparation
* Orders ready for delivery
* Delivered orders
* Payment status

---

### Inventory control

Inventory must accurately reflect operational events such as:

* Purchases
* Production
* Sales
* Adjustments
* Waste

Managers require complete traceability for every inventory movement.

---

### Performance during peak hours

Lunch and dinner rushes generate many simultaneous orders.

The application must remain responsive while minimizing the number of interactions required from employees.

---

## Product Positioning

This system is **not** intended to become a generic enterprise ERP.

Instead, it focuses on becoming an operational tool specialized for restaurants where:

* Speed is the primary business objective.
* Tablet-based operation is preferred.
* Kitchen communication is critical.
* Inventory must remain synchronized with restaurant activity.
* Employees need intuitive interfaces requiring minimal training.
