# 6. Business Rules

This chapter defines the global business rules that govern the Restaurant Management System. These rules apply across multiple modules and represent the canonical business behavior of the application.

---

# 6.1 Order Lifecycle

Every order shall follow the operational lifecycle below:

```text
Pending
    ↓
Ready
    ↓
On the Way
    ↓
Delivered
    ↓
Paid
```

Each state represents a completed operational milestone.

| State      | Description                                                                             | Responsible Role       |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Pending    | Order has been confirmed and is waiting for kitchen preparation.                        | Waiter                 |
| Ready      | Kitchen has completed preparation and the order is waiting to be collected.             | Kitchen Personnel      |
| On the Way | A waiter has collected the order from the kitchen and is delivering it to the customer. | Waiter                 |
| Delivered  | Every item belonging to the order has reached the customer.                             | Waiter                 |
| Paid       | Every customer basket associated with the order has been completely paid.               | Waiter / Administrator |

---

## Allowed State Transitions

The following transitions are valid:

* Pending → Ready
* Ready → On the Way
* On the Way → Delivered
* Delivered → Paid

The following transitions require special permissions:

* Pending → Cancelled
* Ready → Cancelled

Transitions that skip intermediate states shall not be allowed.

---

# 6.2 Order Persistence

A confirmed order shall be persisted immediately.

Persistence shall occur before any downstream operation, including:

* Kitchen notification
* Thermal printing
* Dashboard updates
* Reporting
* Payment tracking

Orders shall never exist only in application memory.

---

# 6.3 Order Editing

Pending orders may be modified after confirmation.

Supported modifications include:

* Adding products
* Removing products
* Updating quantities
* Changing product modifications
* Updating observations

Every modification shall generate an audit record.

Kitchen displays shall automatically receive updated order information.

---

# 6.4 Order Cancellation

Orders may only be cancelled by users holding the appropriate permission.

Cancelling an order shall:

* Require explicit confirmation.
* Record the responsible user.
* Record the cancellation reason.
* Reverse inventory movements generated by the order.
* Generate an audit event.

Cancelled orders shall remain visible in historical reports.

---

# 6.5 Service Locations

Every order belongs to exactly one service location.

Service locations are configurable and may represent:

* Dining tables
* Dispatch windows
* Counters
* Future service areas

Each location shall define whether multiple simultaneous active orders are permitted.

Example:

| Location        | Multiple Active Orders |
| --------------- | ---------------------- |
| Dining Table    | No                     |
| Dispatch Window | Yes                    |

This behavior shall be configurable without software changes.

---

# 6.6 Split Accounts

Orders may contain one or more customer baskets.

The system shall support:

* A single shared basket.
* Multiple individual baskets.
* A combination of shared and individual baskets.

Each basket shall maintain:

* Assigned products.
* Independent subtotal.
* Independent payment status.

An order shall only reach the **Paid** state once every basket has been fully settled.

---

# 6.7 Product Configuration

Products are managed through the product catalog.

Each product defines:

* Category
* Price
* Printer alias
* Available ingredient removals
* Available options
* Recipe reference
* Tax configuration

Only modifications explicitly configured for a product may be selected.

Products with identical configurations shall be grouped into a single order line.

---

# 6.8 Pricing

Product prices are defined in the product catalog.

For the initial deployment:

* All menu prices include the applicable 15% tax.
* Product modifications do not affect pricing unless explicitly configured.
* Order totals shall always equal the sum of their line totals.

Historical orders shall preserve the prices effective at the time of sale.

---

# 6.9 Payment Rules

Payments are independent of order fulfillment.

Customers may consume food before payment is completed.

Each customer basket supports:

* Partial payments.
* Independent payment tracking.
* A single payment method per payment transaction.

Supported payment methods in the initial release are:

* Cash
* Bank Transfer (Bank A)
* Bank Transfer (Bank B)

Payment history shall be immutable.

---

# 6.10 Kitchen Operations

Kitchen personnel are responsible only for food preparation.

Kitchen users may:

* View pending orders.
* Review product modifications.
* Mark orders as Ready.

Kitchen users shall not:

* Modify orders.
* Cancel orders.
* Register payments.
* Access financial information.

---

# 6.11 Waiter Delivery Workflow

The Waiter Delivery Panel introduces operational states intended to improve service speed.

After an order reaches the **Ready** state:

1. The waiter collects the order and marks it **On the Way**.
2. Once every item has reached the customer, the waiter marks the order **Delivered**.

These operational states are intended to:

* Improve communication between kitchen and waiters.
* Reduce forgotten orders.
* Measure delivery performance.
* Provide operational KPIs.

---

# 6.12 Inventory Rules

Inventory is managed through inventory movements rather than direct stock modifications.

Every inventory movement shall reference a business event.

Supported movement types include:

* Purchase
* Production Consumption
* Production Output
* Sale
* Adjustment
* Waste
* Rollback

Inventory reservations are not supported.

Inventory shall only change when the corresponding business event occurs.

---

# 6.13 Production Rules

Production transforms raw ingredients into produced inventory items.

Production shall:

* Consume recipe ingredients.
* Increase produced item stock.
* Generate inventory movements.
* Preserve recipe versions.

Sales shall never consume raw ingredients directly unless the sold product is classified as a resale item.

---

# 6.14 Inventory Rollback

When an authorized operation reverses a business event, the corresponding inventory movements shall also be reversed.

Rollback operations shall:

* Generate new inventory movements.
* Preserve historical records.
* Never modify existing inventory transactions.

---

# 6.15 Auditability

Every significant business event shall be permanently auditable.

Auditable events include, but are not limited to:

* Order creation
* Order modification
* Order cancellation
* Status transitions
* Payments
* Inventory movements
* Production
* Administrative changes
* User role assignments

Each audit record shall contain:

* User
* Timestamp
* Action
* Affected entity
* Previous value (when applicable)
* New value (when applicable)

Audit records shall be immutable.

---

# 6.16 Reporting

Reports shall always be generated from persisted operational data.

Historical reports shall remain consistent regardless of future configuration changes.

Business reports shall never depend on volatile application memory.

---

# 6.17 Authorization

The system shall implement Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).

Permissions shall be assigned to roles rather than individual users.

The initial roles are:

* Administrator
* Waiter
* Kitchen Personnel

Future roles shall be supported without changes to the authorization model.

---

# 6.18 Business Configuration

Operational behavior shall be configurable whenever possible.

Examples include:

* Service locations.
* Payment methods.
* Kitchen warning thresholds.
* Delivery warning thresholds.
* Taxes.
* Restaurant information.
* Thermal printer configuration.
* Menu catalog.

Business rules should be modified through configuration rather than software changes whenever feasible.


# 6.19 Business Invariants

Business invariants define the conditions that must always hold true throughout the system. Regardless of user actions, workflows, or future feature additions, these constraints shall never be violated.

---

## Order Invariants

### BI-001 — Every Order Has a Service Location

Every order shall be associated with exactly one service location.

A service location may represent:

* Dining table
* Dispatch window
* Counter
* Other future service areas

Orders cannot exist without a location.

---

### BI-002 — Every Order Contains Products

An order shall contain at least one order line.

Orders with no products cannot be confirmed.

---

### BI-003 — Order Numbers Are Unique

Each order shall have a unique identifier.

Order numbers shall never be reused.

---

### BI-004 — Confirmed Orders Are Persistent

Every confirmed order shall be stored in the database.

Confirmed orders shall never exist only in application memory.

---

### BI-005 — Historical Orders Are Immutable

Historical business data shall never be physically modified.

Corrections shall be represented through new business events rather than altering historical records.

---

## Order State Invariants

### BI-006 — Valid State Progression

Orders shall only move through valid state transitions.

The system shall reject invalid transitions.

Example:

* Paid → Pending ❌
* Ready → Pending ❌
* Delivered → Pending ❌

---

### BI-007 — Delivered Means Fully Delivered

An order shall only reach the **Delivered** state when every product belonging to that order has been delivered to the customer.

---

### BI-008 — Paid Requires Full Settlement

An order shall only reach the **Paid** state after every customer basket has been fully paid.

---

### BI-009 — Cancelled Orders Are Final

Cancelled orders cannot return to an active operational state.

Any future customer request shall create a new order.

---

## Customer Basket Invariants

### BI-010 — Products Belong to Exactly One Basket

Each order line shall belong to exactly one customer basket.

A product cannot simultaneously belong to multiple baskets.

---

### BI-011 — Basket Totals Must Be Consistent

The total of all customer baskets shall always equal the total value of the order.

---

## Payment Invariants

### BI-012 — Payments Cannot Exceed Outstanding Balance

A payment shall never exceed the remaining balance of the customer basket unless explicitly authorized by an administrator.

---

### BI-013 — Payment History Is Immutable

Recorded payments shall never be edited or deleted.

Corrections shall be represented through additional business events such as refunds or reversals.

---

### BI-014 — Every Payment Belongs to One Basket

Every payment transaction shall reference exactly one customer basket.

---

## Product Invariants

### BI-015 — Historical Prices Are Preserved

Orders shall preserve the product price that was effective when the order was confirmed.

Later price changes shall not affect historical orders.

---

### BI-016 — Historical Recipes Are Preserved

Production records shall reference the recipe version used at the time of production.

Future recipe changes shall not modify historical production data.

---

### BI-017 — Product Configuration Defines Available Modifications

Users may only select ingredient removals and options explicitly defined for the product.

---

## Inventory Invariants

### BI-018 — Every Stock Change Generates an Inventory Movement

Inventory quantities shall never change without creating a corresponding inventory movement.

---

### BI-019 — Every Inventory Movement Has a Business Origin

Each inventory movement shall reference the business event that generated it.

Examples include:

* Purchase
* Production
* Sale
* Waste
* Adjustment
* Rollback

---

### BI-020 — Inventory Cannot Become Negative

Inventory shall never become negative unless explicitly permitted by system configuration.

---

### BI-021 — Inventory History Is Immutable

Inventory movements shall never be edited or deleted.

Corrections shall always generate new inventory movements.

---

## Audit Invariants

### BI-022 — Every Critical Business Event Is Auditable

Every critical business event shall generate an audit record.

Examples include:

* Order creation
* Order modification
* Order cancellation
* Status transitions
* Payments
* Inventory movements
* Production
* Administrative changes

---

### BI-023 — Audit Records Are Immutable

Audit records shall never be modified or deleted.

---

## Authorization Invariants

### BI-024 — Every User Has a Role

Every authenticated user shall be assigned at least one role.

Users without a valid role shall not access operational modules.

---

### BI-025 — Permissions Are Granted Through Roles

Business permissions shall never be assigned directly to users.

Permissions shall be inherited from roles.

---

## Reporting Invariants

### BI-026 — Reports Use Persisted Data

Reports shall always be generated from persisted operational data.

Temporary application state shall never be used for reporting.

---

## Configuration Invariants

### BI-027 — Business Behavior Is Configuration-Driven

Operational behavior shall be configurable whenever possible.

Configuration changes shall not require application code changes.

---

### BI-028 — Historical Configuration Is Preserved

Changes to products, prices, recipes, taxes, or restaurant configuration shall not modify historical operational records.

Historical reports shall always reproduce the business state that existed when each transaction occurred.
